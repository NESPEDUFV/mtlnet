            % clause. Every claim, number and hedge is the Resumo's; the external-baseline

            \noindent Location-based social networks produce \emph{check-in} records
            that associate a user, a point of interest (POI), and a time. From these
            records, a model can classify the category of a POI and can predict
            properties of a user's next visit, such as its category and its region.
            This dissertation investigates whether multitask learning (MTL) can
            combine these tasks in a single model, and on which design choices the
            success of joint training depends. The tasks share data and context, but
            sharing parameters between them can also cause negative transfer. The
            investigation comprises three successive studies: a negative result, its
            diagnosis, and its resolution. The first study proposed MTLnet, a joint
            model with a POI-level representation and hard parameter sharing, trained
            for category classification and next-category prediction. Despite the
            sharing, the joint model did not consistently outperform the models
            dedicated to each task. The second study kept the MTLnet architecture and
            changed only its input, replacing the single POI embedding with decomposed
            spatial, temporal, and categorical encoders. Category performance improved
            markedly across the evaluated datasets, which indicated that, in that
            configuration, the input representation was the main limiting factor.
            Based on this diagnosis, the third study moved from a POI-level to a
            check-in-level representation, with one vector per visit rather than per
            POI, replaced hard sharing with a cross-attention topology, and paired two
            sequential tasks: next-category and next-region prediction. Next region,
            the official neighborhood-scale unit where the next visit occurs, is
            treated as an end target of equal standing with next category rather than
            as an auxiliary signal; the nearest published models do not study this
            pairing, and the external baselines were adapted so that a region
            comparison exists at all. The final model was evaluated on six datasets
            from different geographic contexts, including one non-U.S. dataset, with
            user-disjoint cross-validation and paired statistical tests.
