# AI-detection landscape + credibility research (2026-07-20)

> Raw research record for reviewer persona 16 (AI-credibility). Complements ai_writing_evidence_2026-07-18.md (tells/failure modes) — this file covers detectors, non-native bias, human perception, and disclosure-credibility practices.

## detector_landscape_2026

MARKET STATE (mid-2026): The detector market has bifurcated. Legacy perplexity-era tools (Turnitin AI, GPTZero classic mode, ZeroGPT) are being abandoned by universities, while a new active-learning generation (Pangram) is being institutionally adopted by VENUES and ADMISSIONS rather than classrooms.

TURNITIN: Officially claims <1% document-level FPR (docs >=300 words; sentence-level ~4% per third-party analysis: https://gradpilot.com/news/ai-detector-false-positive-rates-compared). Scores carry ±15pp stated variance. Independent June-2026 study in Research Integrity and Peer Review (120 full-length dentistry manuscripts, 6 detectors) found Turnitin's SENSITIVITY has collapsed on new models: 66.7% on GPT-4.5, 10% on GPT-4o, 3.3% on DeepSeek-R2 — i.e., Turnitin now mostly misses modern AI text while retaining FP risk; Originality.ai topped the study (100%/90%/100% on the same sets) (https://originality.ai/blog/ai-detection-studies-round-up, study coverage via https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319137/20260626/ai-text-detectors-flag-polished-human-writing-ai-new-studies-expose-built-paradox.htm). A study in Int. J. Educational Integrity found 15-26% FPR on human text in realistic conditions (https://www.eyesift.com/blog/ai-detection-for-students/).

UNIVERSITY ABANDONMENT (12+ institutions as of Mar-2026 tracker, none re-enabled): Vanderbilt (2023, calculated 750 false accusations/yr at 1% FPR: https://www.vanderbilt.edu/brightspace/2023/08/16/guidance-on-ai-detection-and-why-were-disabling-turnitins-ai-detector/), Pittsburgh, Boston U, Georgetown ("harms of false positives worse than advantages"), Yale ("unsuitable for high-stakes"), Johns Hopkins (advisory-only), UT Austin, MIT Sloan ("AI detectors don't work"), UBC, U Toronto, U Waterloo (Sep 2025 — internal tests flagged human text as "100% AI"), Manchester ("unreliable and biased"), ANU, Curtin (Jan 2026, "education, not surveillance") (https://gradpilot.com/news/colleges-that-disabled-ai-detectors, https://tohuman.io/blog/universities-banning-ai-detection-2026). Nature ran a 2026 feature questioning how well these programs work (https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01358-2).

PANGRAM (the new benchmark): Univ. of Chicago Booth audit (Jabarian & Imas, BFI WP 2025-116) found Pangram essentially zero FPR/FNR on medium-long passages, the only tool meeting a 0.5% policy cap without losing power; Originality.ai and GPTZero <=1% on medium-long, 2-3% on short; RoBERTa baseline flags 30-69% of human text (https://bfi.uchicago.edu/insights/artificial-writing-and-automated-detection/, https://gradpilot.com/news/ai-detector-false-positive-rates-compared). Pangram self-reports ~0.01% overall FPR, ~0.004% on academic essays (https://www.pangram.com/blog/all-about-false-positives-in-ai-detectors). Now the default screener in some college-admissions workflows (https://gradpilot.com/news/pangram-labs-default-ai-detector-college-admissions-2025) and used by NeurIPS 2026 (below).

GPTZERO: Multiclass "Mixed" class since mid-2023; self-benchmarks 99.4% (v4.1b, 2026) and 96.5% on mixed docs, but independent 2,400-sample field testing found ~87% accuracy with ~10% FPR; accuracy drops on short passages, lightly-revised AI drafts, and FORMAL ACADEMIC PROSE BY NON-NATIVE SPEAKERS (https://gptzero.me/news/gptzero-ai-detection-benchmarking-the-industry-standard-in-accuracy-transparency-and-fairness/, https://undetectable.ai/blog/gptzero-accuracy-rate/, https://mpgone.com/is-gptzero-accurate-our-2025-test-results-here/).

HYBRID HUMAN+AI-EDITED TEXT (the dissertation's exact case) is where ALL detectors fail worst, in both directions: (a) APT-Eval / "Almost AI, Almost Human" (Saha & Feizi, Findings of ACL 2025; 14.7K samples, 12 SOTA detectors): detectors frequently flag even MINIMALLY AI-polished human text as AI-generated, cannot differentiate degrees of AI involvement, and are biased against text polished by older/smaller models (https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1303/, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15666); (b) conversely, standard detectors misclassify AI-polished text as fully human 10-75% of the time depending on tool. NeurIPS 2026's own calibration showed the window-size dependence: default 250-350-word windows put 42.7% of position papers in high-AI range; ~100-word windows dropped it to 12.7% — i.e., hybrid-text scores are threshold- and windowing-artifacts, not stable measurements (https://blog.neurips.cc/2026/06/02/ai-generated-papers-in-the-neurips-2026-position-paper-track/). NeurIPS also verified Pangram did NOT flag permissible proofreading/light copy-editing, but did flag substantive generation from minimal human input — the best available evidence that disclosed copy-edit-level AI use survives the strongest current detector.

VENUE ENFORCEMENT (relevant to a CS author's ecosystem): NeurIPS 2026 position-paper track desk-rejected 178 submissions (18.4%) at Pangram >=0.9 (or >=0.8 with concerning patterns) and gave 123 (12.7%) a chance to show evidence of human authorship (accepted evidence = version histories with pre-AI/post-AI/final checkpoints). ICLR 2026: undisclosed extensive LLM use = desk rejection; detectors used only for TRIAGE, action only on concrete AC/SAC-verified evidence (https://blog.iclr.cc/2025/11/19/iclr-2026-response-to-llm-generated-papers-and-reviews/). ICML 2026 desk-rejected 497 papers (~2% of submissions) tied to 398 reviewers who violated LLM-review rules (https://blog.icml.cc/2026/03/18/on-violations-of-llm-review-policies/, https://blog.icml.cc/2026/01/08/whats-new-in-icml-2026-peer-review/).

IMPLICATION FOR THE PERSONA: the realistic threat model in 2026 is NOT "Turnitin will catch it" (Turnitin barely detects modern models) — it is (1) a Pangram-class screener at a venue/committee scoring hybrid text unpredictably, and (2) human examiners pattern-matching. Detector scores on hybrid text are officially recognized (NeurIPS) as needing corroborating process evidence, which is the author's strongest shield.

## nonnative_bias

FOUNDATION — Liang, Yuksekgonul, Mao, Wu & Zou 2023 (Patterns): 7 GPT detectors on 91 TOEFL essays — average 61.3% falsely flagged as AI; 97.8% flagged by at least one detector; ~near-perfect accuracy on US 8th-grade native essays. Mechanism: detectors read LOW lexical richness / low perplexity (simpler word choice, more uniform syntax typical of L2 writers) as "machine-like." Crucially, when the TOEFL essays were REWRITTEN WITH MORE ELABORATE, AI-style vocabulary, the FPR fell from 61.3% to 11.6% — i.e., the trigger is structural simplicity, not AI-ness (https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666389923001307, https://gradpilot.com/news/ai-detector-false-positive-rates-compared).

REPLICATIONS 2024-2026: the disparity replicates across platforms, populations, and conditions — Giray 2024; Pratama 2025; Gotoman et al. 2025; Lege 2025; Hadra et al. 2026 — establishing it as a category-level property of detection, not a single-tool bug (survey context: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.28749). A 2025-11 arXiv study extends it to slightly-polished Arabic text (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16690). Aggregate FPRs on non-native academic writing of 5-12% (peer-reviewed/university studies) up to 61% (worst tools) are cited in 2026 syntheses (https://www.eyesift.com/blog/ai-detection-for-students/).

NEWEST AND STRONGEST — Pindrop + Authors Guild, ACL 2026 (San Diego, Jul 2-7, 2026; preprint arXiv 2512.09292): audited 16 detection systems (zero-shot + trained, incl. Ghostbuster, Glimpse) for bias across gender, race, English-learner status, income. Clearest failure was demographic: ESSAYS BY ENGLISH-LANGUAGE LEARNERS DISPROPORTIONATELY FLAGGED across most systems; effects are INTERSECTIONAL — non-White male ELL writers hit hardest; gender/race alone minor but pronounced when combined with language status. Expert HUMAN annotators in the same study were 45-53% accurate (coin flip) but showed NO statistically significant demographic bias — humans are inaccurate but not demographically skewed; machines are the biased channel (https://www.pindrop.com/article/ai-text-detection-bias/).

HUMAN-RATER BIAS: "The Great AI Witch Hunt" (Hadan et al., Computers in Human Behavior: Artificial Humans 2024; 17 top-tier HCI reviewers) found reviewers' AI-suspicion heuristics were unreliable and their "language snippet" suspicions contradict the detector-bias literature — reviewer folk-detection penalizes exactly the limited-linguistic-proficiency features L2 authors produce; judgments were CONSISTENT but not ACCURATE (confidence without validity) (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949882124000550, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12015).

COUNTER-SIGNAL: Pangram re-tested the original Liang TOEFL set and reports 0.00% FPR — a first-party vendor claim, but consistent with the independent Booth audit's near-zero FPR finding (https://gradpilot.com/news/ai-detector-false-positive-rates-compared).

IMPLICATIONS FOR A BRAZILIAN AUTHOR WRITING IN ENGLISH: (1) He faces a DOUBLE bind: L1-interference simplicity triggers detectors, while AI-polish to fix it triggers human "reads like ChatGPT" perception — the two failure modes have OPPOSITE cures, so the persona must audit for both separately. (2) Never let anyone treat a detector score as evidence; the Liang + Pindrop line is the citable defense, and 12+ major universities' abandonment statements are institutional precedent. (3) Protection = provenance, not style: keep the full PT-BR→EN drafting chain (Portuguese notes/outlines/drafts are near-unforgeable authorship evidence), Overleaf/Google Docs version history, prompt logs, and per-chapter checkpoints matching the NeurIPS pre-AI/post-AI/final evidence format (https://blog.neurips.cc/2026/06/02/ai-generated-papers-in-the-neurips-2026-position-paper-track/). (4) A disclosure statement pre-agreed with the advisor/committee converts any later suspicion from an integrity question into a documented-process question (UGA/McGill-style policies: https://grad.uga.edu/policy-on-use-of-generative-ai-in-theses-and-dissertations/, https://www.mcgill.ca/gps/thesis/thesis-guidelines/use-generative-ai-research). (5) Do NOT "write worse English" to seem human — the Liang rewrite result shows richer, more varied prose LOWERS detector FPR, and examiners penalize weak prose anyway; the target is idiosyncratic-but-competent, not simple.

## human_perception_triggers

WHO CAN ACTUALLY TELL: Russell et al., ACL 2025 ("People who frequently use ChatGPT for writing tasks are accurate and robust detectors of AI-generated text", arXiv 2501.15654): annotators who heavily use LLMs are near-perfect — majority vote of 5 experts misclassified 1 of 300 articles, beating most commercial detectors even against paraphrase/humanizer evasion. Their cues: "AI vocabulary" lexical tells PLUS harder-to-fake gestalt properties — formality, originality, clarity ("too clean," "too even") (https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.267/, https://www.forbes.com/sites/dereknewton/2025/04/21/study-shows-experienced-humans-can-spot-text-created-by-ai/). CS examiners in 2026 are precisely this population — assume expert-level human detection, not coin-flip (naive readers remain ~45-53%: https://www.pindrop.com/article/ai-text-detection-bias/).

STRUCTURE-LEVEL TELLS (the most current, field-tested catalog is Wikipedia's WP:Signs of AI writing, continuously updated through Jul-2026 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing): (1) RULE-OF-THREE overuse (triadic adjective/phrase lists everywhere); (2) uniform paragraph length and outline-like section rhythm; formulaic "challenges and future prospects" moves ("Despite its X, Y faces challenges..."); (3) bullet-itis: vertical lists with bold-header-colon pattern ("**Header**: description") where prose belongs; (4) wrap-up/summary sentences appended to sections and the essay-style outline conclusion; (5) NEGATIVE PARALLELISM ("not just X, but Y"; "not X, but Y"); (6) copula avoidance ("serves as," "functions as," "boasts," "offers" instead of "is"); (7) present-participle vague-significance trailers ("...highlighting the importance of," "underscoring"); (8) vague attribution ("observers note," "experts argue"); (9) em-dash proliferation (usage in scientific abstracts more than doubled 2021-2025; some editors count dashes as a tell — https://nickpotkalitsky.substack.com/p/why-ai-cant-stop-using-em-dashes, pushback: https://www.theringer.com/2025/08/20/pop-culture/em-dash-use-ai-artificial-intelligence-chatgpt-google-gemini); (10) mechanical boldface, title-case headings, elegant-variation synonym cycling (repetition-penalty artifact); (11) puffery/significance-inflation vocabulary (pivotal, testament, underscores, vibrant, landscape, delve, intricate, interplay).

WHAT REVIEWERS REPORT SUSPECTING (venue culture): the Witch Hunt study (17 HCI reviewers) — AI-suspected text "lacks research details and reflective insights from authors," loses "human touch"/subjective voice; suspicions consistent but unreliable, and reviewers act on them anyway (https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12015). At scale, Organization Science (Apr 27, 2026; 6,957 submissions Pangram-scored over 5 years) found manuscripts crossing ~30% AI-share were sharply more likely to be desk- and post-review-rejected, EVEN when the AI writing scored HIGH on complexity — polished-sounding language now signals lower scientific value to gatekeepers; submission readability overall fell 1.28 SD below Jan-2021 level (https://source.washu.edu/2026/05/analysis-reveals-ais-impact-on-research-journals/, https://phys.org/news/2026-05-journal-ai-academic-publishing-quality.html). Venues now formalize suspicion: ICLR 2026 desk-rejects undisclosed extensive LLM use; NeurIPS 2026 desk-rejected 18.4% of position papers; NeurIPS planted hidden-prompt traps to catch AI-written REVIEWS (telltale phrases like "This work addresses the central challenge"), itself controversial (https://www.the-scientist.com/a-trap-for-ai-use-in-peer-reviews-sparks-controversy-74702, https://www.thetransmitter.org/publishing/scientists-decry-conferences-use-of-hidden-prompts-to-snare-ai-peer-reviews/).

KEY ASYMMETRY the persona must encode: DETECTORS are triggered by low-perplexity simplicity (hurting L2 writers); EXPERT HUMANS are triggered by high-gloss uniformity — evenness of rhythm, frictionless transitions, absence of concrete first-person research detail, and lexical tells. Fixes for one can worsen the other; audit both channels independently. Also note perception heuristics are EXPLOITABLE and drifting: humans hold known-flawed heuristics (Jakesch-line finding echoed in 2025-2026 work), and human prose is itself converging on LLM style ("Human-LLM Coevolution," arXiv 2502.09606), so tell-lists decay — structure/discourse-level signals (uniformity, no reflective specificity) outlive word-lists.

## credibility_protection

DISCLOSURE: HELPS OR HURTS? The 2025-2026 evidence is consistent: disclosure carries a measurable trust penalty, but it is manageable and asymmetric. (1) Schilke & Reimann 2025, "The transparency dilemma: How AI disclosure erodes trust" (OBHDP; 13 experiments): disclosed AI users are trusted less than non-disclosers, via reduced perceived LEGITIMACY; effect holds across framings, voluntary vs mandatory, beyond algorithm aversion — BUT being EXPOSED by a third party after nondisclosure is worse than disclosing, and positive-AI-attitude audiences show smaller penalties (https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/the-transparency-dilemma-how-ai-disclosure-erodes-trust/, https://blog.scielo.org/en/2025/10/10/the-transparency-paradox-when-using-generative-ai-in-academic-research/). (2) EMNLP 2024 (arXiv 2410.04545): the penalty is concentrated on disclosed CONTENT-GENERATION assistance; disclosed proofreading/language polish draws far smaller devaluation — so the WORDING of the disclosure (editing vs generating) materially changes reception (https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.279/). (3) "Full Disclosure, Less Trust?" (arXiv 2601.09620, Jan 2026): one-line disclosures had minimal trust impact; DETAILED disclosures reduced trust yet ~2/3 of readers wanted the detail — recommended pattern is layered "detail-on-demand": short statement up front, full appendix behind it (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09620). (4) Reader-perception study (arXiv 2510.24011; 261 participants, 990 responses): disclosure erodes trustworthiness/competence/likability MOST in social-interpersonal writing, least in informational writing; HIGHER AI LITERACY OF THE READER MITIGATES OR REVERSES the penalty — favorable for CS examiners (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24011). (5) JCOM 2026 finds parallel credibility effects in science communication (https://jcom.sissa.it/article/pubid/JCOM_2501_2026_A09/).

INSTITUTIONAL DISCLOSURE PRACTICE (2026 norm for theses): disclose the TOOL, the TASK, the LIMITS, and demonstrated author control; two-layer format (short front-matter statement + fuller appendix) is emerging best practice; committee pre-approval where required (UGA: committee-authorized scope + in-document statement; Illinois State: disclose all use to committee; KU Leuven: GenAI statement mandatory even for language-only help; McGill GPS guidelines effective May 1, 2026) (https://ai-cards.org/ai-disclosure-thesis/, https://grad.uga.edu/policy-on-use-of-generative-ai-in-theses-and-dissertations/, https://www.mcgill.ca/gps/thesis/thesis-guidelines/use-generative-ai-research, https://libguides.princeton.edu/generativeAI/disclosure, https://www.thesify.ai/blog/gen-ai-policies-update-2025).

PROVENANCE AS THE REAL SHIELD: the NeurIPS 2026 appeal protocol is the template — keep pre-AI, post-AI, and final checkpoints per chapter showing the human verified/authored substance (https://blog.neurips.cc/2026/06/02/ai-generated-papers-in-the-neurips-2026-position-paper-track/). Student-defense guidance converges: timestamped edit chains (Google Docs/Word 365/Overleaf history; Draftback replay), outlines and notes, prior writing samples establishing personal style, and readiness for oral defense of any passage (https://gptzero.me/news/falsely-accused-of-ai-cheating/, https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Composition/Specialized_Composition/AI_and_College_Writing:_An_Orientation/03:_Academic_Integrity/3.03:_How_can_I_protect_myself_against_false_accusations_of_using_AI, https://www.analyticsinsight.net/artificial-intelligence/how-to-prove-your-work-isnt-ai-generated-a-practical-guide, https://hub.paper-checker.com/blog/oral-defense-viva-preparation-prove-authorship-ai-accusation/). For this author specifically, the Portuguese-language thinking trail (PT-BR outlines, advisor emails, meeting notes) is high-value provenance no generator produces incidentally.

VOICE PRESERVATION / WHAT NOT TO OVER-CORRECT: (1) Do not strip every em-dash, transition word, or "moreover" — blanket tell-scrubbing produces the "defensive writing" pattern Wikipedia's guide explicitly warns renders judgments unreliable, and 2025-2026 commentary documents an em-dash moral panic penalizing legitimate usage (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing, https://www.theringer.com/2025/08/20/pop-culture/em-dash-use-ai-artificial-intelligence-chatgpt-google-gemini). (2) Do not simplify vocabulary to "sound human" — simplicity is what triggers detectors against L2 writers (Liang 61.3%→11.6% when enriched). (3) The properties experts key on are gestalt — uniform formality, frictionless clarity, zero reflective specificity — so the highest-yield edits are ADDITIVE, not subtractive: inject concrete research detail, first-person methodological choices and dead-ends, varied paragraph/sentence rhythm, occasional plain "is" statements, and honest hedges tied to actual results (per Witch Hunt reviewers' "lacks research details and reflective insights"; per Russell et al. experts' formality/originality/clarity cues). (4) Keep the disclosure statement's framing accurate but task-precise ("language editing and revision under author direction" if true) — the EMNLP 2024 result shows generation-framing draws the penalty, and honesty matters because exposure-after-nondisclosure is the worst outcome (Schilke & Reimann). (5) The witch-hunt authors' venue recommendation doubles as the credibility anchor: authorship = maintained control and accountability over the text; demonstrate that, and disclosed AI assistance is defensible under every 2026 venue policy (ICLR/ICML/NeurIPS all permit disclosed copy-editing).

## whats_new_vs_july2026_baseline

Substantial genuinely-new material since ~May 2026 that a Kobak/Matsui/Shaib/QUDsim-centric baseline would miss — mostly on the DETECTOR-INSTITUTIONAL and PERCEPTION side, not the stylometric side: (1) NeurIPS 2026 position-paper track (blog Jun 2, 2026) — first major venue to run Pangram (v3.3.2) screening at scale with published thresholds (>=0.9 desk-reject; >=0.8 + patterns), outcomes (178 desk-rejects = 18.4%; 123 = 12.7% asked for authorship evidence by Jun 15), the window-size sensitivity finding (42.7%→12.7% flagged when windows shrunk to ~100 words), verification that light copy-editing does NOT flag, and a formal appeal protocol built on version-history checkpoints — this is now the canonical evidence-of-authorship format (https://blog.neurips.cc/2026/06/02/ai-generated-papers-in-the-neurips-2026-position-paper-track/). (2) Organization Science study (published Apr 27, 2026; WashU/press coverage May 2026; Nature/FT/Forbes pickup) — 6,957 submissions + 10,389 reviews Pangram-scored: rejection cliff at ~30% AI-share, AI-heavy papers rejected even with high writing-complexity scores, >30% of peer reviews contain AI assistance, submission readability down 1.28 SD since 2021 — first journal-scale quantification of the reviewer-side "polish penalty" (https://source.washu.edu/2026/05/analysis-reveals-ais-impact-on-research-journals/, https://phys.org/news/2026-05-journal-ai-academic-publishing-quality.html). (3) Pindrop + Authors Guild 16-detector demographic-bias audit, presented at ACL 2026 (Jul 2-7, San Diego; arXiv 2512.09292) — the major post-Liang bias update: intersectional ELL bias (non-White male ELL worst), humans 45-53% accurate but demographically unbiased (https://www.pindrop.com/article/ai-text-detection-bias/). (4) June 2026 Research Integrity and Peer Review dentistry-manuscript study — documents Turnitin's sensitivity collapse on 2025-26 models (10% on GPT-4o, 3.3% on DeepSeek-R2) vs Originality.ai near-100%, i.e., the legacy-detector obsolescence is now peer-reviewed (coverage: https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319137/20260626/ai-text-detectors-flag-polished-human-writing-ai-new-studies-expose-built-paradox.htm). (5) "Full Disclosure, Less Trust?" (arXiv 2601.09620, Jan 2026, likely absent from a stylometric baseline): one-line disclosure ~costless, detailed disclosure costs trust yet readers want it → layered detail-on-demand disclosure design. (6) Fresh policy motion: McGill GPS thesis-GenAI guidelines effective May 1, 2026; Curtin disabled detection Jan 2026; GradPilot Mar-2026 tracker consolidates 12+ abandonments with zero re-enablements; Nature 2026 feature on detector reliability (https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01358-2). (7) New arXiv detection work Jun 2026: test-time-adaptation detection under continual distribution shift (arXiv 2606.25152), explainable detection "Show, Don't TELL" (arXiv 2605.27921), and "Why AI-Generated Text Detection Fails: Evidence from Explainable AI" (arXiv 2603.23146) — collectively reinforcing that detector scores are unstable under drift and hybrid editing. (8) ICML 2026 Mar-18 blog: 497 papers desk-rejected over reviewer LLM-policy violations; NeurIPS hidden-prompt traps for AI reviews sparked ethics controversy (The Scientist, The Transmitter) — reviewer-side AI policing is now reciprocal and punitive. NOTHING fundamentally new on the stylometric-tells side beyond the baseline (word-lists, templates, discourse skeletons remain as of Jul 2026); the Wikipedia WP:AISIGNS page has continued absorbing 2026 model generations (GPT-5, Grok, DeepSeek citation artifacts) and is the most current human-tell catalog.
