## ai_tells_update

KEY 2025-2026 FINDINGS BEYOND THE CLASSIC WORD LISTS:

1) Word lists are rotting — target distributions, not tokens. Kobak et al., "Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications through excess vocabulary," Science Advances 11(27):eadt3813 (2025): 15M PubMed abstracts 2010-2024; 379 style words with elevated 2024 frequency; lower-bound estimate >=13.5% of 2024 abstracts LLM-processed (up to 40% in some subcorpora) [https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adt3813, code: https://github.com/berenslab/llm-excess-vocab]. Matsui, Perspectives on Medical Education (Dec 2 2025, PMC12679996): 27.5M PubMed records; 103/135 AI-associated terms exceeded modified-Z>=3.5 in 2024; top risers "delve, underscore, primarily, meticulous, boast"; crucially documents DELIBERATE AVOIDANCE since March 2024 — "delve, intricate, realm, pivotal" are now declining as authors learned the tells, and some rises predate ChatGPT (2020) [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12679996/]. Implication for a writing law: a static banned-word list has a shelf life; add a rule that the list is versioned and refreshed, and that the deeper detectable signal is distributional.

2) POS-profile and register tells (survey-level consensus). Terčon & Dobrovoljc, "Linguistic Characteristics of AI-Generated Text: A Survey" (arXiv 2510.05136, Oct 2025): across studies, LLM text shows lower lexical diversity, smaller vocabulary, repetitive text, MORE nouns/determiners/adpositions (nominal style), FEWER adjectives/adverbs, and a more formal impersonal register; evidence is mostly GPT-family and English [https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05136]. Actionable check: noun-density / nominalization ratio per section, not just word bans.

3) Syntactic templates — repetition at the part-of-speech level. Shaib et al., "Detection and Measurement of Syntactic Templates in Generated Text" (arXiv 2407.00211): models emit favored POS-tag sequences at higher rates than human reference text; 76% of model templates trace to pre-training data vs 35% for human text; NOT removed by RLHF [https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00211]. Follow-up: Shaib et al., "Measuring AI 'Slop' in Text" (arXiv 2509.19163, Sep 2025, rev. Jan 2026): expert-interview-derived taxonomy of "slop"; binary slop judgments correlate with latent coherence and relevance dimensions; introduces span-level annotation plus template-rate/templates-per-token and compression-ratio (words and POS) metrics [https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19163]. QUDsim (arXiv 2504.09373) quantifies discourse-structure similarity: LLMs reuse the same discourse skeletons across generations even when content differs — the mechanism behind "every chapter opening feels the same" [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09373].

4) Tells are MODEL-SPECIFIC ("aidiolects") — a guardrail tuned only to ChatGPT-isms misses Claude/Gemini-isms. Em-dash overuse is a ChatGPT-specific tell; Claude and Gemini are measurably more restrained (Rolling Stone [https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-features/chatgpt-hypen-em-dash-ai-writing-1235314945/]; The Conversation [https://theconversation.com/too-many-em-dashes-weird-words-like-delves-spotting-text-written-by-chatgpt-is-still-more-art-than-science-259629]). "Detecting Stylistic Fingerprints of Large Language Models" (arXiv 2503.01659): Claude/Mistral have global fingerprints, Gemini a context-dependent one. Stylometric fingerprinting in role-constrained settings reaches 97.1% five-way model attribution (arXiv 2606.09854). Reference-quality corpus comparison of per-model word rates: refsmmat.com — e.g., Claude over-uses "genuine/genuinely" at 2.19/1.55 per 1,000 words, ~10x rivals [https://www.refsmmat.com/notebooks/llm-style.html].

5) The deepest new tell is VARIANCE COMPRESSION, not any word: "Voice Under Revision" (arXiv 2604.22142) shows LLM revision homogenizes personal voice — Claude Sonnet reduced variance in 78% of stylometric features (median reduction 26%), GPT-5.x compressed 60%, Gemini 3.x 74%. So uniform sentence/paragraph statistics across a whole dissertation is itself the signature; a guardrail should mandate preserving the author's variance (burstiness), not just avoiding words [https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22142v1].

Counterpoint worth citing for calibration: "Almost Nobody Is Using ChatGPT to Write Academic Science Papers (Yet)" (PMC12382442) argues full-text generation in published science remains rarer than headline claims — the dominant real-world mode is assisted editing, which is exactly the co-writing regime the dissertation law governs [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12382442/].

## longform_failure_modes

MEASURED FAILURE MODES THAT ONLY APPEAR AT LENGTH:

1) Repetition and degradation with output length. LongGenBench (arXiv 2409.02076v7): across 9 advanced models with 32K-128K contexts, long-form generation shows premature task termination, incomplete responses, instruction disregard, and repetitive content; ~45% of long outputs exhibited significant repetition, and repetition_penalty tuning barely helps [https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02076v7]. HelloBench (arXiv 2409.16191): most LLMs cannot produce quality text beyond ~2,000 words; long-text-tuned open models (LongWriter-GLM4-9B, Suri-I-ORPO) reach length but with severe repetition and quality collapse [https://arxiv.org/html/2409.16191v1]. Also LongEval (plan-based long-text analysis, arXiv 2502.19103) and "Shifting Long-Context LLMs Research from Input to Output" (arXiv 2503.04723) document that output-side length is the under-solved axis. Guardrail implication: never draft a chapter in one pass; generate in bounded sections against an outline, with near-duplicate detection (compression-ratio / n-gram overlap across chapters — the Shaib slop metrics) run over the whole document.

2) Context rot / lost-in-the-middle makes cross-chapter consistency structurally hard. Context degradation is an architectural property of transformer attention: U-shaped position sensitivity (strong at beginning/end of context, weak in the middle); strikingly, models retrieve WORSE from logically coherent documents than shuffled ones — coherent text creates plausible distractors [https://www.morphllm.com/context-rot; https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/context-degradation-in-large-language-models]. Ref-Long (arXiv 2507.09506) benchmarks long-context referencing specifically and finds models fail to track which document/section supports a claim [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09506]. Implication: an agent "keeping the whole thesis in context" will silently miss mid-document constraints (notation defined in ch. 2, claim scoped in ch. 3); consistency must be enforced by external artifacts (glossary, notation table, claim registry), not by context window.

3) Whole-paper generation studies quantify document-level fabrication. PaperRecon / PaperWrite-Bench (arXiv 2604.01128, U. Tokyo, Apr 2026): agents regenerating 51 recent papers from an overview show a presentation-hallucination TRADE-OFF — ClaudeCode produced higher presentation quality but >10 hallucinations per paper on average; Codex fewer fabrications but weaker presentation. Polish anti-correlates with groundedness: the best-looking draft is the most dangerous [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01128]. Independent evaluation of Sakana's AI Scientist (arXiv 2502.14297 + coverage): 57% of generated papers (4/7) contained incorrect or hallucinated numerical results; structural errors included missing figures, placeholders, DUPLICATED SECTIONS, median 5 mostly-stale references; 42% experiment failure rate [https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14297; https://byteiota.com/ai-scientist-v2-passes-peer-review-but-57-is-false-data/].

4) Fake cohesion: QUDsim (arXiv 2504.09373) and syntactic-template work (arXiv 2407.00211) show LLMs recycle discourse skeletons — chapters that "flow" identically (same move structure, same transition sentences) are template reuse, not argumentation. Detectable as high cross-section discourse similarity.

5) Notation/terminology drift across a multi-chapter document: NO direct benchmark exists yet (evidence gap worth stating in the law). Nearest evidence: the LLM-consistency literature documents low output stability and terminology disagreement (Consistency in Language Models, arXiv 2505.00268); linguistic properties of the SAME task drift across model updates (MDPI Electronics 15(6):1262 — GPT-4.1 vs GPT-4o produce measurably different lexical sophistication), so a dissertation written over months with rolling model versions will drift in register mid-document [https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/15/6/1262]; and LLM-based multi-artifact consistency verification is emerging as its own QA task (Koli Calling 2025, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3769994.3770042). Practical rule: maintain a single machine-readable notation/glossary file (the project already has GLOSSARY.md-style practice) and lint every chapter against it; sciwrite-lint (see number_integrity) already checks dangling cross-references, orphan references, abstract-vs-body and caption-vs-content mismatches — i.e., the long-form internal-consistency class is now automatable [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08501].

## citation_integrity

MEASURED RATES (2025-2026):
- Corpus-scale: Columbia team led by Maxim Topaz, The Lancet (May 2026; via STAT): >2M papers / 97M citations screened; ~4,000 fabricated citations across ~2,800 papers; prevalence rose from 1 in 2,828 papers (2023) to 1 in 458 (2025) to 1 in 277 (first 7 weeks of 2026) — a >12-fold, sixfold-then-accelerating rise; >1/3 of fabricated citations concentrated in two large open-access publishers; est. ~146,932 hallucinated citations in 2025 alone (~51/10,000 papers late 2025, 56.9/10,000 early 2026) [https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/07/lancet-study-finds-steep-rise-fraudulent-citations-academic-papers/; https://phys.org/news/2026-05-ai-generated-fake-citations-scientific.html; https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-00969-z].
- Model-level: JMIR Mental Health 2025 (PMC12658395): GPT-4o fabricated 19.9% of citations in simulated literature reviews, ranging 6% (well-covered topic) to 28-29% (sparse topics) — fabrication scales with topic sparsity, so niche dissertation subfields are highest-risk. GPT-5 WITH web search still ~7-8% fabricated (domain-dependent) [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12658395/; https://www.inra.ai/blog/citation-accuracy]. Deep-research agents also hallucinate references (arXiv 2604.03173) [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.03173].
- Venue-level: GPTZero audit found 100 hallucinated citations in 53 ACCEPTED NeurIPS 2025 papers (~1% of accepted), undetected by 3-5 reviewers each; Ansari's taxonomy (arXiv 2602.05930, Feb 2026): Total Fabrication 66%, Partial Attribute Corruption 27%, Identifier Hijacking 4%, Placeholder 2%, Semantic 1%; 100% exhibited compound failure modes; bimodal contamination — 92% of affected papers had 1-2 (light AI use), 8% had 4-13 (heavy reliance) [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.05930; https://fortune.com/2026/01/21/neurips-ai-conferences-research-papers-hallucinations/]. ~300 ACL/NAACL/EMNLP 2024-25 papers had >=1 hallucinated citation (almost all 2025); 20% of sampled ICLR 2026 submissions contained >=1 AI hallucination [https://library.smu.edu.sg/topics-insights/arxiv-tightens-policy-hallucinated-references-what-researchers-should-know-about].
- Journal-side audit: 12% of 500 submissions to a mid-tier medical journal had >=1 reference unverifiable against CrossRef/PubMed [https://www.journalmetrics.org/blog/ai-fabricated-citations-medical-research-2026].

REAL CONSEQUENCE CASES: Springer Nature retracted the book "Mastering Machine Learning: From Basics to Advanced" (Aug 2025) after Retraction Watch found 2/3 of 18 spot-checked citations nonexistent or substantially wrong [https://retractionwatch.com/2025/08/04/springer-nature-retracts-book-fake-citations-help-us-find-more/]. arXiv now bans authors for UP TO ONE YEAR when there is incontrovertible evidence of unverified LLM content (hallucinated refs, residual prompts) — announced May 2026 [https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01595-5; https://www.insidehighered.com/news/faculty/books-publishing/2026/05/22/ban-authors-who-submit-ai-content-welcome-unenforceable]. An Accountability in Research paper argues hallucinated citations can constitute RESEARCH MISCONDUCT when citations function as data [https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08989621.2026.2645390].

VERIFICATION WORKFLOWS ACTUALLY ADOPTED: Springer Nature runs Geppetto (AI-generated-text detector) + SnappShot + a 2025 AI tool flagging IRRELEVANT references across nearly all its journals/books via its Research Integrity Group [https://group.springernature.com/gp/group/media/press-releases/new-research-integrity-ai-tool/27769148; https://www.springernature.com/gp/researchers/the-source/blog/blogposts-research-integrity/protecting-research-integrity-new-ai-tools/27385130]. Elsevier expanded "Check Integrity" screening to ~2,000 journals (auto-flags to human integrity analysts) [https://www.elsevier.com/about/press-releases/elsevier-expands-article-submission-screening-tool-to-strengthen-research]. NEJM and JAMA use citation-validation tools; Science family reports zero published fabricated-citation incidents (per STAT). Proposals: mandatory automated citation verification at submission (arXiv 2602.05930); full-text reference deposit (arXiv 2503.19848); multi-agent citation-hallucination detection (arXiv 2605.08583). DISSERTATION-LEVEL RULE THIS SUPPORTS: no reference enters the .bib unless resolved via DOI/arXiv ID against Crossref/PubMed/OpenAlex AND the PDF was opened AND the cited claim was located; LLMs never write bib entries from memory; run a final adversarial pass that spot-verifies claim-support, not just existence (existence checks miss the 27% "partial attribute corruption" and 63% secondary "semantic hallucination" classes).

## number_integrity

EVIDENCE OF THE RISK: LLMs corrupt numbers EVEN WHEN GIVEN CORRECT INPUT — faithfulness (contradicting the provided source) is distinct from factuality, and numeric spans are a top faithfulness-error class in summarization: "From Single to Multi: How LLMs Hallucinate in Multi-Document Summarization" (NAACL Findings 2025) [https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.293.pdf]; documented behaviors include citing the right dataset with the wrong figure and "correcting" a provided drug dosage to a different value [https://www.getzep.com/ai-agents/reducing-llm-hallucinations/]. At paper scale: Sakana AI Scientist evaluations found 57% of generated papers contained incorrect/hallucinated numerical results, including impossible 95-100% accuracies on deliberately noise-corrupted data [https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14297]; PaperRecon measured >10 hallucinations/paper for the most polished agent (arXiv 2604.01128). So drift/fabrication around real experimental results is the expected default, not an edge case.

PRACTICES WITH EVIDENCE OR FORMAL GUARANTEES:
1) Fail-closed numeric verification at the presentation layer: Proof-Carrying Numbers (arXiv 2509.06902, Solatorio, Sep 2025) — every numeric span is emitted as a claim-bound token checked by a verifier OUTSIDE the model against a declared policy (exact/rounded/tolerance); only verified numbers get a mark; unverified numbers fail closed. Formally proven sound/fail-closed. The pattern to copy: "trust is earned only by proof" — any number in prose without a machine-checkable pointer to the source table is treated as unverified [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.06902].
2) Draft-side linting: sciwrite-lint (arXiv 2604.08501, Samsonau, Apr 2026) checks numbers-in-text vs tables, abstract vs body, figure captions vs content, statistical results vs their verbal interpretation, plus dangling citations/orphan references; runs locally on open-weights models; validated by error injection on 30 unseen papers. This is the first tool explicitly built for "science vibe-writing" QA [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08501].
3) Single-source-of-truth generation (pre-LLM reproducibility practice, now doubly load-bearing): showyourwork! builds every figure, table, and \variable macro programmatically from the pipeline so no result number is ever hand-typed into LaTeX [https://github.com/showyourwork/showyourwork; https://show-your.work/en/latest/latex/]; same pattern via Makefiles [https://github.com/jpreiss/reproducible_papers] and "Reproducible Science with LaTeX" (arXiv 2010.01482). Adapted to human+agent co-writing: (a) one canonical results file (e.g., the project's RESULTS_TABLE.md §0) is the ONLY permitted numeric source; (b) agents may QUOTE numbers verbatim but never compute, round, aggregate, or "recall" them; (c) a diff-based audit extracts every numeral+unit from the chapter and matches it against the canonical table (exact or declared-rounding match), failing the build on any orphan number — i.e., a poor-man's PCN implementable today with a script; (d) derived quantities (deltas, percentages) must be computed by code committed to the repo, then imported, never by the model in-prose.

## process_guardrails

GUARDRAIL PATTERNS AND THEIR EVIDENCE BASE:

1) Provenance-per-span tracking (who wrote what): HaLLMark (CHI 2024, arXiv 2311.13057) — interactive provenance highlighting of AI-written vs AI-influenced text + prompt timeline; study with 13 writers showed it increased sense of control/ownership and willingness to disclose accurately [https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3641895]. DraftMarks (arXiv 2509.23505, 2025) extends this with process traces. Evidence level: HCI user studies. Practical form for a dissertation: keep AI-drafted vs human-drafted status per section in git (separate commits/branches per origin) so the disclosure statement is reconstructible rather than remembered.

2) Automated verification gates before human review: the direction the ecosystem actually adopted in 2025-2026 — publisher-side (Springer Nature Geppetto + irrelevant-reference detector across nearly all journals; Elsevier Check Integrity in ~2,000 journals, auto-flag -> human analyst), venue-side (arXiv negligence-based 1-year ban; mandatory automated citation verification at submission proposed after the NeurIPS-2025 audit, arXiv 2602.05930), and author-side (sciwrite-lint local linter with error-injection validation; PCN fail-closed numeric verification). Evidence: deployment at scale + the Lancet/GPTZero audits showing human peer review alone missed 100% of the fabrications it later found. Rule: no chapter passes to advisor review until the lint gate (citations resolve, numbers match canon, cross-refs resolve) is green.

3) Diff-based audits / adversarial passes: Retraction Watch's 18-citation spot-check forcing a Springer retraction, and GPTZero's post-acceptance NeurIPS audit, are existence proofs that a cheap adversarial spot-audit catches what generative review misses. sciwrite-lint's error-injection methodology doubles as a self-test: inject known errors, confirm your audit pass catches them, then trust it. The bimodal contamination finding (92% of affected papers 1-2 hallucinations, 8% with 4-13; arXiv 2602.05930) supports scaling audit intensity with the share of AI-drafted text in a chapter.

4) Claim whitelists / claim registries: no 2025-2026 academic literature uses the term, but three lines converge on it: (a) PCN's claim-binding contract (every displayed claim bound to a verifiable source); (b) the Accountability in Research argument that citations function as DATA, making unsupported claims misconduct-adjacent [https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08989621.2026.2645390]; (c) faithfulness benchmarks (FaithBench, NAACL 2025) separating "contradicts source" from "contradicts world." A dissertation claim-whitelist (the project's CLAIMS_AND_HYPOTHESES.md pattern) is best framed as claim-level provenance: every scientific claim in prose must map to a whitelisted claim ID with its evidence pointer; agents may not introduce claims outside the registry. Evidence level: analogical + formal (PCN), not yet RCT-grade.

5) Human sign-off gates with explicit accountability: ICMJE (Apr 2025) — humans responsible for all AI-assisted material; arXiv — author negligence standard with bans; UGA — committee PRE-authorization of scope before any AI use; U. Toronto SGS — disclosure to committee. These establish that the enforceable unit is the human gate, not the tool. Also AI Usage Cards (https://ai-cards.org/ai-disclosure-thesis/) as a standardized disclosure artifact.

6) Anti-slop editorial pass distinct from fact pass: Shaib slop taxonomy (arXiv 2509.19163) + syntactic-template metrics give measurable targets (template rate, compression ratio, discourse similarity via QUDsim) for a style audit separate from the integrity audit; "AI-Slop to AI-Polish" (arXiv 2504.07532) shows edit-based rewriting passes measurably improve writing quality. Keep the two passes separate: a fact/number/citation gate (fail-closed) and a style/variance gate (statistical).

## disclosure_policies

PUBLISHERS (2025-2026 state; all four prohibit AI authorship):
- ICMJE (updated April 2025): disclose AI use AT SUBMISSION, in the cover letter AND in the appropriate section of the work; chatbots cannot be authors; humans responsible for all AI-assisted material [https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/ai-use-by-authors.html]. COPE and WAME aligned.
- Elsevier: mandatory disclosure in a dedicated statement placed before the references; generative AI for images prohibited; AI only to improve readability, with human oversight [https://scipubplus.com/hub/blog/elsevier-vs-springer-nature-comparing-ai-policies-for-academic-authors/].
- Springer Nature: document LLM use in the Methods section (or suitable alternative); explicit carve-out — "AI-assisted copy editing" needs NO disclosure, generative drafting does; enforced by in-house screening (Geppetto).
- IEEE: disclosure in the Acknowledgments section stating (i) which AI system, (ii) which sections used it, (iii) the level of use.
- ACM: generative AI may not be listed as author; any AI-created content must be fully disclosed in the Work.
- arXiv (May 2026): authors accountable; up to 1-year ban for unverified AI content (hallucinated refs, residual prompts) [https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01595-5].

## paragraph_shape_and_cohesion_2026-08-21

Requested by the author after he personally noticed two patterns in AI-drafted prose: (1) nearly
every paragraph opens with a short, decontextualized, strong claim that the rest of the paragraph
unpacks; (2) long documents lose connective words both within and between paragraphs, so each one
reads as an isolated block. Each source below was individually opened (WebFetch on the arXiv
abstract/HTML page or the Wikipedia page) and its key sentence quoted directly — not taken from a
research sub-agent's paraphrase, per this file's own R1/R2 discipline.

1) Claim-first paragraph shape, confirmed. Kim, Chang, Pham & Iyyer, "Argument Collapse: LLMs
Flatten Long-Form Public Debate" (arXiv 2606.01736, submitted 2026-06-01; 23,384 essays, NYT/Boston
Review debates vs. LLM output). Verbatim: "LLM-generated essays tend to follow a more fixed arc,
often opening with a direct claim and moving quickly toward proposals." Measured: within one
debate, main-argument uniqueness 65.3% (human) vs 3.4% (LLM); sub-argument uniqueness 41.0% vs
9.1%; "LLMs often reuse generalized and hedged sub-arguments, while humans prefer more concrete and
topic-specific ones" — i.e. the elaboration behind the opening claim is generic, not
evidence-driven. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01736]

2) Connective-word loss, measured at corpus scale, with a CS-specific finding. Bao, Zhao, Mao &
Zhang, "Examining Linguistic Shifts in Academic Writing Before and After the Launch of ChatGPT: A
Study on Preprint Papers" (arXiv 2505.12218, 2025; 823,798 arXiv abstracts). Verbatim: cohesion and
readability "indicates that abstracts have fewer connecting words" post-ChatGPT, alongside greater
lexical complexity but simplified sentence structure; "scholars in Computer Science showed more
pronounced changes in writing style, while the changes in Mathematics were minimal" — directly
relevant since this dissertation is CS. Caveat from the wider survey literature (Terčon &
Dobrovoljc 2510.05136, already cited above): the defect is not always zero connectives — AI text
can also over-rely on formulaic, swappable transitions ("moreover," "furthermore"), so the sharper
test is whether a transition is load-bearing or decorative, not merely whether one is present.
[https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12218]

3) Sentence length without local repair. Gude, Santos-Ríos, Bond, Flickinger, Gómez-Rodríguez &
Zamaraeva, "More Aligned, Less Diverse? Analyzing the Grammar and Lexicon of Two Generations of
LLMs" (arXiv 2605.06030, submitted 2026-05-07; HPSG/ERG-parsed comparison of 2023- vs
2025-generation LLMs against human-authored NYT text). Verbatim (via the HTML rendering): "In
2023, human sentences were about 10-20% longer than LLM sentences. In contrast, newer models
produce sentences that are 15-30% longer than sentences written by human authors" (Table 7: 2023
LLMs ~18-19 tokens/sentence, 2025 LLMs ~25-30, humans ~22 both years); yet "despite generating
longer sentences, newer LLMs do not appear to create more complex structures... they are easier for
the ERG to parse" (parse success: humans 93-95%, 2023 LLMs 96-97%, 2025 LLMs 98-99%) — longer
sentences that are grammatically too clean, lacking the local repair/re-phrasing real (especially
non-native) prose carries. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06030]

4) Rigid section/paragraph skeletons, reconfirmed live (community-maintained, checked 2026-08-21,
not a peer-reviewed source but corroborates the discourse-skeleton-reuse literature already cited
above with concrete named shapes). *Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing*: a "Challenges" section that
always opens "Despite its [positive framing], X faces challenges..."; a present-participle ("-ing")
phrase tacked onto a sentence's end as decorative/superficial elaboration (same family as the
already-banned participial significance tails); "headings only containing other headings"; "rule
of three" overuse reconfirmed still live; inline vertical bullet lists standing in for connected
