\paragraph*{Errata: o controle de concatenacao (Cap.~5, p.~79).}
A frase corrigida le:

\begin{quote}
A feature-concatenation control adds the per-visit features to the place embedding and feeds the
result to the same single-task model. On the scale of Table~9, this control closes most of the gap
between the two representations, and at one dataset it overshoots. At Alabama it raises the place
embedding by $1.73$ macro-F1 points (paired over five folds, $p = 0.003$, every fold in the same
direction), which covers the whole $1.56$-point gap; at Arizona by $1.70$ points ($p < 0.001$), or
$68$ percent of the $2.50$-point gap; and at Florida by $1.02$ points ($p < 0.001$), which is nearly
five times the $0.21$-point gap there. Arizona is the one dataset where the check-in-level
representation still leads the concatenation arm, by $0.80$ points ($p = 0.03$); at Alabama the two
are within $0.18$ points ($p = 0.53$), and at Florida the concatenation arm leads by $0.81$ points
($p = 0.001$). The category gain therefore comes mostly from the per-visit features that the
representation reads, and not from the hierarchical structure that reads them. What Table~9
establishes is that the input representation is the dominant factor, which this control supports; it
does not establish which part of the representation carries the gain.
\end{quote}

\noindent
A frase substituida afirmava que o ganho vem da representacao hierarquica e nao de injecao de
features. Medido na mesma escala, e a injecao de features que carrega a maior parte do ganho de
categoria, e em dois dos tres datasets ela alcanca ou supera a representacao por check-in. A
conclusao central do capitulo nao muda, porque ela e sobre a representacao de entrada contra a
arquitetura, e nao sobre qual componente da representacao contribui mais.
