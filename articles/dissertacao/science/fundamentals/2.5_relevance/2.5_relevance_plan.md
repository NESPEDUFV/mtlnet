## Argument skeleton (synthesis of 2.1-2.4)
1. The three tasks are distinct (2.1): predicting the next **category** and the next **region** is not the same
   problem as predicting the exact next **place**, and treating them as co-equal end targets is what this work does.
2. The representation is the lever (2.2): the field moved one-hot -> place embeddings (DGI -> HGI), but a place-level
   vector is static across visits; the check-in level is the response.
3. Multi-task learning is not free (2.3): naive hard sharing can fail to beat single-task models (negative transfer),
   and elaborate gradient balancers frequently do not beat a tuned fixed-weight baseline.
4. Honest evaluation is the arbiter (2.4): user-disjoint cross-validation, macro-F1 / Acc@10 / MRR / Δm against named
   floors and the dedicated single-task ceiling, with verbs bound to paired tests and TOST.

## The "pressing need" hinge paragraph (ends 2.5)
One paragraph whose three clauses each pre-motivate one paper chapter, in order:
- **Clause 1 -> Ch.3 (CBIC):** does naive multi-task learning even help, given a place-level embedding and hard
  sharing? (Sets up the honest null result.)
- **Clause 2 -> Ch.4 (CoUrb):** if it does not, is the *representation* the lever rather than the architecture?
  (Sets up decomposing/enriching the input.)
- **Clause 3 -> Ch.5 (MobiWac, submitted/under review):** what does a representation built for *check-ins* unlock
  for a redesigned joint model? (Sets up Check2HGI + the joint model that finally wins category everywhere and
  region at four of six datasets with TOST non-inferiority at the other two.)
